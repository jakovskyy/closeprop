The title search

A participating title company searches the public record before your seller lists, for old liens, unreleased mortgages, boundary questions and a name on the deed that does not match. Found before the listing, there is time to clear them. Found in contract, any one of them can delay the closing or end the deal.

The report and the badge

Both results come back as the Verified Seller report, dated and attached to the property. It goes to your seller, to you and to your seller’s attorney. When it reaches the deal, Tilo offers to summarize it for you. The badge goes on the listing when your seller lists on ClosingMap, so a buyer can see the work was done before they write an offer.

How sponsoring works. You answer one question, "Do you want to sponsor a verified seller?", yes or no. If yes, you cover the whole cost and your seller pays nothing. You pay once the listing agreement is signed. A seller with no sponsor can order and pay for it themselves.

Why sponsor it

The work starts before the listing does.

The report comes back to you and your seller at the same time. The unreleased mortgage from 2009, the roof the inspector flagged, the name on the deed that does not match theirs: you work through them with your seller before the home is listed, and long before a buyer's attorney would have found them.

Before the home is listed, your seller has already watched you solve a problem for them. When a buyer comes, their inspector checks what your seller disclosed, and their title search starts from a record your seller already cleared.

Questions

The ones that come up first.

What am I sponsoring?
A preliminary title search and a pre-listing home inspection on your seller's home, ordered once the listing agreement is signed and done before the home is listed. Both results come back as the Verified Seller report, and the badge goes on the listing when your seller lists on ClosingMap.
How much do I pay?
The whole cost of the search and the inspection. Sponsoring is yes or no, and your seller pays nothing. You see the price before you say yes, and you pay once the listing agreement is signed.
Why would I pay for a seller's title search?
Your seller gets answers about their own home at no cost to them, and you get time working with them before the home is listed. You and your seller decide what to fix and what to offer the buyer as a credit, before a buyer is involved.
Does my seller have to fix everything the search and the inspection find?
No. What the inspector finds, your seller can fix, price in, or offer the buyer as a credit, and the contractors' estimates show what each one costs. A problem on the title record, such as an unreleased mortgage, still has to be resolved before the closing, and finding it early gives your seller time to do it.
Can I make it conditional on getting the listing?
Yes. You pay once the listing agreement is signed, so if your seller lists with someone else, you have spent nothing.
Do I have to sponsor it to work with a Verified Seller?
No. A seller can order and pay for it themselves, and any licensed agent can take a Verified listing, run it and close it with the same search, the same inspection and the same reports. A buyer's agent can bring a buyer to one with nothing asked of them. Sponsoring is how you become the agent who brought it.
Does my plan matter?
No. Sponsorship is separate from Free, Pro and Featured, and it changes nothing about what the seller receives.
Does my seller have to close with the title company that ran the search?
In New York and New Jersey, the buyer's attorney orders the title insurance for the closing, so that choice usually belongs to the buyer's side. The participating title company runs the preliminary search now. If the buyer's side uses that company, it starts from the search already done. If they choose another, the report still stands.
What happens if the home does not sell?
Nothing further is owed, by you or by your seller. The report stays attached to the property, dated, and your seller can still see it.
Does this replace the buyer's inspection?
No. The buyer's inspector works for the buyer, and the buyer should still have one. Their inspector checks what your seller already disclosed, so fewer surprises come up in the middle of a negotiation.
Is the preliminary search the same as title insurance?
No. A preliminary search shows what is on the public record today. It is not a title commitment and it is not a policy. The buyer still buys title insurance. Problems your seller cleared before the listing are already resolved when that search is run.

09Verified Seller

Verified Seller runs a preliminary title search and a pre-listing home inspection on a seller’s property before it goes on the market.

Verification belongs to the seller

Verification attaches to the seller and the property. It does not attach to you. A seller can be verified whether or not their agent has any relationship with us, and any licensed agent can take a verified listing, run it and close it on the same terms as anyone else. A buyer’s agent can bring a buyer to a verified listing with nothing asked of them.

Nothing an agent, attorney, lender or title company pays us affects whether a seller qualifies, what that seller receives, or what that seller is charged. No payment we receive or make is tied to the referral of settlement service business.

Cost, and when it becomes payable

The seller sees the full price and pays when they order, inside ClosingMap. Nothing is owed at closing. An agent may cover part or most of it, and the seller sees that share before ordering.

Open · price, sponsorship and refunds

Not decided: the price, the sponsoring agent’s share and how and when the agent pays it, and what a seller is refunded, if anything.

The charge is identical whichever title company the seller chooses. A seller who brings their own title company pays exactly what a seller who uses a participating title company pays.

What a verified listing does not do

A preliminary title search is information. It is not title insurance and it is not a title opinion. It speaks as at the date it was run, title changes afterwards, and no lender accepts a seller-side search in place of its own requirements.

A pre-listing inspection was commissioned by the seller and reports what the inspector saw on that day. It is not a warranty and it is not a substitute for a buyer’s own inspection.

Buyers should obtain their own inspection and their own title insurance. Nothing in the program, the badge, the reports or the marketing should be read as telling a buyer they can skip either, and you must not present it that way to anyone.

01What this covers

This policy covers CloseProp: the marketing site, the product and the Verified Seller program as it touches people you work with. It is written for two audiences at once, because CloseProp holds two kinds of information.

ClosingMap and TYTLVERSE are separate products with their own policies.

02What we collect

When you sign up

Your name, work email, state, your license number, bar number or NMLS ID, and where you sign up for an organization, the name of that brokerage, firm or lending team.

When you use the product

The transactions you run and everything on them: properties, parties, tasks, deadlines, documents, messages, offers, statuses and the notes you write. Automatically

Device and browser type, IP address, pages viewed, features used, times and dates, and error reports. We use a small number of cookies to keep you signed in and to understand usage. We do not sell advertising against your activity.

From other sources

State licensing and registration records, to verify the credential you gave us. Public property records. Information other parties add to a transaction you are on.

Before you sign up, a record built from the public New York licensing registry may already exist for you, so that you can find and claim it. It holds your name, license number, license status and state, and nothing else. It appears in no list, and it is reached only by searching your exact name.

Tilo reads what is on a transaction so it can answer questions about that transaction. It drafts and it summarizes. It does not send anything on your behalf and it does not make a decision that has a legal or similarly significant effect on anyone without a person acting on it.

Open · does customer data train the model

This is the sentence an attorney will read twice before putting a client file into your product, and it has not been decided. Two drafts, pick one.

05Who can see what

A transaction is shared, and the sharing is by role rather than by negotiation.

  • Your client sees their own journey, their tasks and their documents.
  • An attorney sees the deal, the contract and the title work, and never the buyer’s financing documents.
  • A loan officer sees the loan and the buyer’s financing documents. Only the buyer and their loan officer see those documents.
  • A title company sees the title work.
  • A co-agent sees the deal you share with them.

If your account belongs to a brokerage, firm or lending team, an administrator at that organization can see the transactions run under its seats.

Inside CloseProp, our own staff access customer content only to run the service, to fix a fault or to meet a legal obligation, and that access is logged.
